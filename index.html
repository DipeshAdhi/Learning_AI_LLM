    New here? What am I actually looking at?

    AI models read text in chunks called tokens — roughly three quarters of a word each. Everything the model can see at once has to fit in a fixed space called the context window, and you pay for every token you put in it, every single time you send a message. The red stripes are text that was sent and paid for on every turn without ever being useful. Phase 1 explains this slowly; Phase 2 shows you how to fix it.

    Illustrative figures, priced at $3 per million input tokens. Token counts are modelled, not measured — Phase 2 shows you how to measure your own.

    Why AI keeps forgetting

    A model has no memory of its own. Everything it appears to remember, someone put back in front of it.

    Every message starts it from nothing — no recollection of yesterday's decision, no idea what you agreed an hour ago. That is true of ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini alike; none of them has quietly fixed it. Which means the real skill was never phrasing a clever question. It is deciding what gets loaded, in what order, and what gets dropped first when the room runs out.

    Everything is a file.

    The book comes with a repository, and the repository is half the point. Skills, memory templates, prompts, and evaluations are plain Markdown you can read, copy, and drop straight into a project of your own.

    Nothing here is a screenshot of a technique. If a chapter says a structure saves tokens, the structure is in the tree and you can run it yourself.
